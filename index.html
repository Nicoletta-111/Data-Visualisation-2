Australia's Changing Picture of Physical Activity Across Generations

We know exercise is good for us. Yet across Australia, our daily habits are shifting, whether that's more screen time, more sitting, and for many, less movement. This visualisation explores how physical activity has changed over the years, who is still moving, and who is falling behind. From generational differences to the gap between city and regional Australia, we follow the data to understand what "being active" really means today. The consequences matter: inactivity contributes to chronic disease, mental health challenges, and a heavier burden on our healthcare system. But the story isn't all decline as some groups are finding new ways to stay active.

Which diseases are most linked to physical inactivity?
Data from 2018 shows diseases ranked by their causative contribution from inactivity. This was displayed as a sorted horizontal bar chart, showing diabetes and heart disease as the top contributors.
Proportion of Australians that fail to meet physical activity guidelines
Data from ABS surveys (2011‑12 and 2022) tracks the percentage of Australians not meeting physical activity guidelines across age groups. The line chart reveals that inactivity peaks in early teens, with 89% of 13–14 year olds failing to meet recommendations.
TV viewing: generations diverge, but at what cost?
TV viewing hours for two generations are compared over time using a slope graph. It shows that older adults (65+) increased daily TV time from 3.2 to 5.0 hours, while young adults (18–29) decreased from 2.1 to 1.8 hours, shifting to other forms of screentime instead.
Shift in screen time: Children exceeding screen limits
A stacked bar chart visualises that only 29% of children aged 5–17 meet daily screen‑time limits, meaning the vast majority exceed recommendations. The green and red bars make the gap immediately clear.
